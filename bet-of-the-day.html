How the call is made

Waiting for a book to misprice a line is one way to find value. This page also makes its own number. It rates every team in the NHL, NFL and college football from past results, with an Elo model that credits margin of victory, home field and a pull back toward average each new season. It then compares its win probability to the eight-book market consensus.

  1. Strong bet. Blended EV of 2% or more. Quarter Kelly, capped at 2% of bankroll.
  2. Model pick. Model-only EV of 3% or more, and blended EV at or above zero. One-eighth Kelly, capped at 1%.
  3. No call. Neither clears.

Fit. NFL and college football settings were tuned before 2025, then scored on 2025: log loss 0.636 over 272 NFL games (0.690 for always picking the home rate) and 0.548 over 761 college games (0.676). The NHL settings were tuned on the same 2,897 games they are scored on, so 0.680 against 0.690 is slightly optimistic. These beat a naive baseline. None has been shown to beat the market, and the ledger below is how that gets tested. The model's weight in the blend only goes up if the record earns it.

Lineups. Starting quarterbacks and goalies move prices more than anything the ratings see, so every call carries guards. NFL: the quarterback comes from the depth chart and the injury feed. A quarterback listed out or doubtful gets a fixed backup penalty, and a questionable one means no call. NHL: the goalie comes from DailyFaceoff, shifted by last season's save percentage. An unlisted goalie means no call. No call is made if the model and the market differ by more than 8 points (NHL) or 12 points (football), because a gap that large is usually news the model cannot see. Only moneylines between −300 and +300 qualify. Only games that start today by the Eastern clock count. A call is void if the named starter does not play.
